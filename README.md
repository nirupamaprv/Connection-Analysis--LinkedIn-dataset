# LinkedIn Connections Segmentation

A Jupyter notebook that breaks down my LinkedIn network (1,587 connections) by **company**, **company category**, **role/function**, **seniority**, and **title keywords**, plus a **connection-year timeline**, a **diversity assessment**, and a comparison of the network with my **own career timeline**.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `connections_segmentation.ipynb` | The analysis (segments, diversity, career timeline). Already run, so all charts and tables show without re-running. |
| `connections_segmented.csv` | Every connection with the derived segment columns (no email addresses). Generated by the notebook. |
| `requirements.txt` | Python dependencies. |
| `export1.zip/`, `export2/` | LinkedIn data export (two identical copies). The notebook reads `export2/Connections.csv`. |

## Data source and limitations

- **Source:** LinkedIn's *Get a copy of your data* export (22 Oct 2026). `Connections.csv` is the same in both export folders.
- **Fields available:** first/last name, profile URL, email (only 35 connections share it), company, position, connected date.
- **No location.** LinkedIn's connections export has no location field, so the notebook can't segment by geography. I didn't guess location from company names because it would be unreliable. The **connection-year timeline** takes its place.
- Company and position are each person's **current** values at export time, not their values when we connected.
- About 3% of connections have a blank company or position. They show up as `Unknown`.

## How the segments are built

All segments come from keyword rules (regular expressions) in editable tables in the notebook.

1. **Company normalization** (`COMPANY_ALIASES`): rolls up variants, for example *Amazon Web Services (AWS)* → *Amazon*, *JPMorgan Chase & Co.* → *JPMorgan Chase*, and self-employed or freelance → *Self-employed / Stealth*.
2. **Company category** (`COMPANY_CATEGORIES`): Big Tech · IT Services & Consulting · Financial Services · Education & EdTech · Healthcare & Pharma · Retail, Consumer & Media · Self-employed / Stealth · Other / Startups.
3. **Seniority** (`SENIORITY_RULES`, checked top to bottom, first match wins): Student/Intern → C-Suite/Founder → VP/Head → Director → Manager → Lead/Principal/Staff → Senior IC → Individual Contributor.
4. **Function** (`FUNCTION_RULES`, first match wins, specific before generic): Recruiting & HR, Product Management, Data/Analytics/AI-ML, Design & UX, QA & Testing, Program & Project Mgmt, Sales/Marketing/BD, Finance & Operations, Education & Mentoring, Founder/Executive, Engineering & IT, Consulting & Business Analysis, Management (general), Other.

The notebook lists the most common titles that matched no function rule, which helps when adding keywords. About 7% of connections currently end up as Other or Unknown.

## Is the network diverse? (notebook section 8)

"Diverse" here means **professional** diversity. Location, gender and ethnicity aren't in the export, and I chose not to guess them from names.

**Measures used**
- **Evenness** (normalized Shannon entropy, 0–1): how evenly connections are spread across the groups that exist. 1 = perfectly even.
- **HHI** (sum of squared shares): concentration, the same measure economists use for markets. Below 0.15 = unconcentrated, above 0.25 = concentrated.
- **Top-1 / Top-10 share**: how much the largest groups dominate.

| Dimension | Evenness | HHI | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company | 0.89 | 0.01 | Very diverse. The top 10 employers hold only 24%, and 57% of connections are the only person I know at their company. |
| Function | 0.91 | 0.10 | Diverse. The largest function is under 20%. |
| Seniority | 0.89 | 0.16 | Balanced from IC to C-suite. |
| Industry | 0.72 | 0.28 | **Concentrated.** Among identifiable industries, IT services (31%), finance (26%) and big tech (26%) make up 84%. Healthcare is 4%, retail/media 2%, public sector close to 0. |

**Verdict:** diverse in *who* (companies, roles, levels), narrow in *where* (industries).

## Network vs. my career timeline (notebook section 9)

Each connection is assigned to the career era I was in on the day we connected (eras built from `Positions.csv`). Then pace and mix are compared across eras.

| Era | Connections | Per month | Director+ | Data/AI | Founder/Exec | From then-employer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-Infosys, no role listed (2013–Nov 2017) | 830 | 14.1 | 20% | 7% | 6% | — |
| Freelance data science consulting (Dec 2017–Oct 2021) | 305 | 6.5 | 24% | 16% | 7% | — |
| Fidelity, Sr Mgr → Director (Nov 2021–Aug 2024) | 280 | 8.2 | **43%** | **25%** | 6% | 87 |
| Amazon, Analytics Mgr (Sep 2024–Dec 2025) | 99 | 6.2 | 9% | 18% | 4% | 50 |
| Udacity faculty & mentoring (2026) | 73 | 7.7 | **49%** | 15% | **26%** | — |

**Interpretation**
1. **2015 was a one-off push.** 612 connections (39% of the network) were made that year, about 300 of them in April and May, up to 23 a day. Most were Infosys and IT-services peers, four years after leaving Infosys. That looks like a reconnection drive or contacts import, not people met through work.
2. **Since then, growth has come mostly through employers.** Fidelity and Amazon colleagues make up most of the connections during those jobs. The freelance years are slower and more scattered.
3. **Seniority follows my role.** Director-level work at Fidelity brought in leaders, the Amazon manager role brought in peers, and faculty and mentoring in 2026 brings in founders and executives.
4. **The baseline is 6–8 connections a month**, which is work-driven, not deliberate outreach.
5. **My shift into data and AI shows up in the network:** data/AI share rises from 7% to 16% to 25% as my career moves from analyst to analytics leader.

**Caveats:** company and position are each person's *current* values, not their values when we connected, so "from then-employer" counts are a lower bound. The 2011–2017 gap may just be roles missing from my profile.

**So what:** the gaps worth working on are industry breadth and outreach outside employers. The 2026 shift toward founders and executives is the first change in years that doesn't come from a new job.
